# PCB as code

The schematic and the board are generated by scripts. Never edit the `.kicad_sch` or
`.kicad_pcb` by hand: the next run overwrites it. Change the script, rerun, look.

## Where the library is

- In a project: pinned as a git submodule (convention `tools/leiterbahn`). Scripts do
  `sys.path.insert(0, "<repo>/tools/leiterbahn/src")`, then `from leiterbahn.kicad.board import Board`.
- Otherwise: this plugin ships it. The plugin root is two directories above this skill's
  directory, and the package is in its `src/leiterbahn/`.
- `leiterbahn.kicad` needs KiCad's own `pcbnew` module. Run it with the system Python that KiCad
  installed. `kicad-cli` and the `freerouting` CLI must be on `PATH`.

Signatures: [api.md](api.md). A board script skeleton with the order of passes, and what
changes for four layers, two ground domains and cut-outs: [board-script.md](board-script.md).
A schematic on several sheets: [sheets.md](sheets.md).

## The pipeline

| Step | Script (convention) | Library | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `schematic.py` | `Schematic` | `.kicad_sch`, `intended_netlist.json` |
| 2 | `check.py` | `verify.erc_and_parity` | ERC report, `build/<name>.net`, parity verdict |
| 3 | `board.py` | `Board`, checks, `drc`, `render` | `.kicad_pcb`, DRC report, renders |
| 4 | `fab.py` | `fab.export` | Gerbers, BOM, CPL (see the `jlcpcb-order` skill) |
| 5 | `export_enclosure.py` | `geometry.export` | `board.json` + STEP (for a housing: plugin `gehaeuse`) |

Every part in `schematic.py` declares its nets; the drawing only realises them. Step 2 proves
KiCad's connectivity equals the declaration. `Schematic.overlaps()` must come back empty: text
collisions are the first thing a reader sees.

**Always run step 2 after a schematic change, before step 3.** The board reads the exported
netlist in `build/`, not the schematic. Skipping it once gave a board with the old pin
assignment and `net_conflict` DRC errors that looked like a routing bug.

## Rules learned the hard way

**Placement decides routing.**
- Keep the straight path down from fine-pitch pins free. A resistor 0.5 mm below a module
  pin made the autorouter neck a trace down to 0.19 mm and zig-zag past it. Moving the
  resistor fixed what no routing setting could.
- If two nets must cross, first try swapping the pin assignment (a GPIO, a connector pin, a
  relay contact pair). Swapping two sense GPIOs removed a crossing for the price of one
  firmware constant. Swapping two connector pins removed another.

**Hand-route what the circuit depends on, autoroute the rest.**
- Hand-route anything with a datasheet layout rule: the switcher hot loop, regulator feedback,
  module supply and decoupling, reset and boot. Prove each with `path_length` limits and
  `foreign_copper` in the hot loop.
- Freerouting does not connect to hand-placed vias, and does not join two groups of fixed
  copper. A hand route that ends in a via, or a hand-routed island, leaves an unrouted
  connection. This happened three times on one board: a pull-up fed through a via, a relay
  coil supply, a divider feed. The fix every time was to hand-route the whole branch, pad to
  pad.
- Hand-routing one net changes what the autorouter can do for its neighbours. A straight 12 V
  line pushed a harness line to the back, through a pointless stub and via next to the
  connector. After every hand-route change, render and look at the neighbours too.
- Call `chamfer_corners()` once, after all hand routes. Chamfering inside `track()` cut
  corners that other routes join at, and left those routes unconnected.

**Clean up what the autorouter leaves.** The order matters ([board-script.md](board-script.md)):
`remove_redundant()` (duplicates of hand routes) → `widen()` (neck-downs) → `straighten()`
(micro-jogs) → pours → `remove_redundant(nets=["GND"])` (ground tracks the pour covers) →
`stitch()` → `prune_dangling()`.
- All passes must agree on what "connected" means. KiCad counts overlapping copper as
  connected. One pass removed short joint segments because the neighbours overlapped; another
  accepted only end-to-end joints, then deleted whole traces piece by piece. Fixed board
  connections vanished. Both now follow KiCad's rule.
- When a count goes wrong, print `unrouted()` after every pass. That found the pass above in
  one rerun; reading the DRC report had pointed at the wrong nets.

**Keep the ground plane whole.**
- Long bottom-layer runs slit the plane. Put `keepout(..., tracks=True, vias=False,
  fills=False)` under the switcher and the module: tracks banned, stitching vias still
  allowed.
- Corner keepouts (fiducials) can cut an edge strip of pour off into an island. Look at the
  poured render, not only the tracks.

**DRC clean is not the same as well routed.** Run the checks DRC lacks:
- `path_length` limits taken from datasheets;
- `foreign_copper` in the switcher hot loop;
- `narrow_tracks` on autorouted copper (neck-downs);
- `acute_angles` (etch traps, duplicate loops);
- `kinks` (steps aside and turns back the router left: legal, and what a reviewer sees first);
- `coupling` (quiet tracks beside noisy copper). Name the nets that swing (switch nodes,
  boot and gate nets, transformer and rectifier nodes, clocks) and those that take up what
  is near them (feedback, compensation, sense, reference, amplifier inputs) once, give both
  lists to `negotiate(quiet=, noisy=)` and to `coupling()`: DRC passes with a sense line
  0.11 mm beside a switch node, and the router only keeps apart what it was told to.
  `intent.roles(b.pin_info, b.nets)` gives both lists as far as pin names tell (FB, COMP,
  amplifier inputs; SW, BOOT, gate drives, clocks); add what only you know (a transformer's
  nets, a clock behind an isolator).
- `Placer.unmet()` right after placement: supply pins with no capacitor within 4 mm,
  feedback and input nodes that reach further than 10 mm, boot capacitors far from their
  pin, all from what the symbols say of the pins. It found on its own what a reviewer had
  found by measuring (a regulator's input 20 mm from its capacitor). `Placer.mend()` moves
  what has room nearer its pin; fix the rest (`first()`, `tie()`, or the schematic: a part
  between the inputs of two distant parts cannot be near both) before routing, which does
  not move parts.
- Pours on outer layers that are added after routing: name them in `negotiate(pours={"GND":
  (F_Cu, B_Cu)})`, or the router leads tracks past through-hole ground pins at clearance
  and the pour then reaches them by one spoke (DRC: starved thermal).
- `b.glance()` before a long routing run, and to choose between placements: a few rounds, a
  fifth of the time or less. Compare by `settled` after 16 rounds: the count of a single
  round swings by a third. One board, eight placements: five settled at 0 to 57 cells in
  conflict, three at 142 to 167, and those three took 136 to 172 rounds to route. `where` names the
  pins, `in_way` the parts beside them, `heat` every square the router fought over.
- `negotiate(race=8)` where processors are free: eight runs with the nets in other orders,
  and the one that is clean in the fewest rounds wins. A board that takes 100 rounds one
  way takes 18 another, and nothing tells beforehand which.
- A placement that settles high has parts in the tracks' way, and neither wire length nor
  pad density shows which: `place.ease(judge, p.loose())` asks the router. It takes the
  parts `glance()` names, moves each out of its place in turn (`p.evict()`: usually to the
  other side) and keeps the move that leaves the fewest cells in conflict. `judge(out)` is
  the board script up to the glance, with `p.evict(*t)` for each `t` of `out` after the
  placement; put the list it returns into the script. On that board one resistor below a
  row of controller pins made the three slow placements slow.

Then render front and back, with and without the pour, and look before showing anyone. The
`layout-reviewer` agent does an independent pass.

## A schematic that reads as a circuit

`Schematic(..., autowire=True)` and a `frame()` around each functional block: `finish()`
wires the nets inside a block and puts a label only where a net leaves it. Supply nets keep
their power symbols.

- Do not show a schematic that is connected by labels alone. The first complete schematic
  of a 480-part board was ERC-clean with parity 0 and was sent back: "Wire separate blocks
  inside with real wires and only connect blocks through labels." Feedback loops, dividers
  and which resistor sits in which line cannot be read from labels.
- The router only follows the placement. With the passives of a block stacked in a column
  next to the IC, every net was correct and the page looked like a wiring loom. Place each
  part where it sits in the circuit: a series resistor in line with its pin, a pull-up above
  the line, a divider as a vertical pair, the feedback network above the amplifier.
- Leave 2.54 mm between two part ends that face each other. A pull-up and a pull-down placed
  end to end on one point gave "autowire: no path to R3.1": each pin's way out was the other
  part's body.
- At an IC with a part on almost every pin, fan the pins out to a row of parts and give the
  pin nearest the row the nearest part. A part hung on an upper pin's line crossed the lines
  of all pins below it; with the nested order no two wires cross.
- A rail local to one block (a controller's VCC) is better a power symbol than a wire: put
  it in `power_symbols`. A pull-up from a pin in the middle of the IC to a wired rail at the
  top has to cross everything between.
- Redrawing must not change the netlist. Before re-placing parts, store for every net the
  sorted list of (part value, pin) and compare after each step; reference numbers shift
  when parts move, values and pins do not.
- A wrong pin type in a library symbol can make a correct circuit fail ERC. KiCad's LM5175
  has the output-sense pin as an output: with the power flag the rail needs, ERC said "Pins
  of type Output and Power output are connected". The fix was a project copy of the symbol
  with that pin as input, not removing the flag.

## Several sheets

`page = sch.sheet("UART", "uart.kicad_sch")` gives a page with the same drawing API.
`sch.save()` writes the root and every sheet; `sch.netlist()`, `sch.overlaps()` and
`erc_and_parity` on the root cover the whole design. Snippet: [sheets.md](sheets.md).

- A net crosses sheets by its name, nothing else, and the library writes every label as a
  global label once a design has sheets. With local labels, `SIG` on the root and `SIG` on a
  sheet were two nets: parity said "declared net SIG ... not found as one KiCad net", ERC
  said "Label connected to only one pin" twice.
- One `power_flag()` per supply net in the design, not one per sheet. A flag on the root and
  a regulator output on a sheet, both on +3V3, gave the ERC error "Pins of type Power output
  and Power output are connected". A flag on the root does drive the power symbols on every
  sheet.
- A project symbol library needs `sch.write_lib_tables(dir)` and a `.kicad_pro` next to the
  root sheet before ERC runs. With the table but no project file, kicad-cli did not read the
  table: ERC warned "The current configuration does not include the symbol library 'proj'"
  for every part from it.

## Four layers and separate grounds

`Board(..., layers=4, planes=[pcbnew.In1_Cu])`. Snippet:
[board-script.md](board-script.md#four-layers-two-ground-domains-cut-outs).

- List the pour-only inner layers in `planes`. They get KiCad's layer type "power" and the
  DSN for Freerouting then says `(layer In1.Cu (type power))`; with the type "mixed" it
  still said `signal`. This was read in the exported DSN. Freerouting has not been run on a
  four-layer board from this library yet: after the first `autoroute()`, look at every inner
  layer before trusting it.
- Give each ground its own region with `zone(net, layer, rect=...)` or `polygon=...`, then
  prove the isolation on the saved board with `min_gap(board, nets_a, nets_b)` and fail the
  build below the limit. DRC does not: a resistor of ground A that ended up in ground B's
  region got B's pour around its pad at the pour's own 0.3 mm clearance, DRC had no
  clearance error, and `min_gap` returned 0.299 mm at that pad. `place_near()` had put it
  there; it knows nothing about domains.

## Many parts, both sides

`leiterbahn.kicad.place.Placer` places by connections on both sides; `Board.place_near()`
only knows the front and one spot. What a 491-part, 100 × 90 mm board with two ground
domains taught:

- Add up the courtyards before drawing an outline: every part once, through-hole parts
  twice, divided by twice the board area. At 100 × 70 mm that board came to 68 %, which is
  not routable with through vias; the size was renegotiated before any placement. Around
  50 % placed without trouble.
- Fix by hand what the circuit decides: connectors, switching loops, the pass transistor,
  shunts, anything that crosses an isolation gap. The placer minimises wire length: left
  alone it put a bypass FET next to the output relay 40 mm from its shunt, because one of
  its nets ended there.
- After `large()` and `small()`, `improve()` moves, turns and exchanges what they placed. On
  a 65 × 56 mm board with 287 parts it took 12 % off the wire length estimate and, routed,
  12 % off the track length. It knows nothing of routing: of eight seeds three routed
  without an open connection, five left 1 to 11 open. Two causes, each found by looking
  at what stayed open: a ground via of `fan_to_planes(lane=0)` in front of a pin (ask
  `shut_in()` after the vias are set: it names such a net in seconds), and a capacitor in
  front of a row of fine-pitch pins. With `fan_to_planes(lane=0.5)` and `Placer(lane=0.3)`
  all eight routed. Give the script a mode that places and routes without writing
  anything, run several seeds through it, and keep the best that routes clean. Weights (`weights={net: 3}`) are worth using here: three nets at a controller
  went from 16 to 2 mm, 8 to 1 mm and 18 to 4 mm.
- `improve(spread=True)` does not begin from where `large()` and `small()` put the parts
  but from where forces put them: connections pull, courtyards push each other apart.
  On the same board the cost ended 9 % lower than without with every one of eight seeds,
  and routed the tracks were 7 % shorter. But the forces take the anchors at their word,
  where `near()` takes the nearest free spot. Two blocks had their anchors on one spot:
  `large()` had set the first part down there and the second, an inductor, 17 mm from
  its converter (a switch node of 30 mm that nobody had noticed, since it routed). The
  forces put the inductor on its anchor, opposite a QFN on the other side, and 7 of 8
  seeds left pins of the QFN open: their vias had nowhere to come out. So before
  `spread=True`: no two blocks on one anchor; a block's anchor where its largest part has
  room (the inductor found none within 13 mm of its second anchor either); and `keep()`
  the side opposite a fine-pitch part free by about 1 mm round its courtyard. With all
  three, 8 of 8 seeds routed, the tracks 12 % shorter than without the forces and the
  switch node 5 mm long. Print the length of such a net in the script: it had routed at
  30 mm without a complaint.
- A board that routes and passes DRC can still be placed badly, and nothing says so. On
  the same board a review by eye found the boot capacitors 8 mm from their pins and on
  the other side, a feedback resistor 34 mm from the controller, regulator output
  capacitors at a test point 20 mm away, and bulk capacitors grounded through 10 mm of
  0.127 mm track because their ground pad lay over the other ground's plane. So: give
  every plane that covers part of the board to `over()`; put ground nets in a net class,
  so that a pad the planes do not reach gets a track of some width; print `strays()` and
  the length of each switch node and feedback node after placing; and have the
  `layout-reviewer` agent look at every placement that is to be kept.
- Round a fine-pitch part not everything can be nearest. Say the order: `first()` places
  the few parts that must win (supply and boot capacitors, what hangs on a feedback node)
  at their pins before anything else and keeps them there; `tie()` says where a part
  belongs when the netlist does not show it (a clock's series resistor at the oscillator,
  the parts of a network that feeds a feedback pin). Then review again: the first round
  of fixes made 6 of 27 findings worse.
- A block's anchor is where its most sensitive net ends, not where there happened to be
  room: the network that feeds a controller's feedback pin had its anchor 25 mm from the
  controller, and no weight on the net could shorten it, since the net was as long as
  the way between the two blocks.
- Where one part sits can matter more than any router setting. A buck-boost controller
  6 mm nearer its power stage: its 13 sensitive nets 22 % shorter, the board routed in 22
  rounds instead of 96. The half perimeter estimate follows what a net would cost alone on
  the board closely (correlation 0.95 over 20 routed placements) and the routed result
  with all nets only loosely (0.6 to 0.7): compare placements by routing them.
- Say where parts may go with `inside()`, `only()` and `keep()`, not with functions in
  `rules`: `improve()` cannot see into a function.
- Run `large()` for every group before `small()` for any. Group by group, the resistors of
  the first groups took the front and a 11 × 7 mm transformer found no room within 45 mm.
- Reserve the room of hand routes with `keep()` before the automatic passes, and the spots
  of their vias on both sides ("FB"). Without that the first DRC had a ground via in a
  capacitor on the back and a sense track through a controller's pad.
- Keep parts in their domain with a rule on the courtyard box (`p.rules`), with the gap
  included. Fixed parts ignore rules, so the parts that straddle the gap are fixed.
- The default clearance has to be smaller than the smallest pad gap of the footprints
  (0.14 mm for a 3.3 mm VSON FET, 0.15 mm for 0.5 mm pitch VSSOP). At 0.15 mm Freerouting
  reported 38 "pin-to-pin clearance violations" before routing anything.
- Freerouting does not connect pins to a plane. With pours on `In1.Cu` exported as planes,
  273 of the 465 connections left open after a pass were on the three plane nets, and more
  passes did not lower the count. Call `b.fan_to_planes()` after the pours and hand routes
  and before `autoroute()`: each ground pad gets its via, and the router no longer sees
  those pins. A pad you routed by hand is left alone, so a star point stays one.
- Freerouting 2.5 starts with a fanout stage that gives every SMD pin a via (1168 on that
  board), where they block the other side. `autoroute()` switches it off; `fanout=True`
  brings it back. Its first start also writes `~/.config/freerouting/freerouting.json`
  with telemetry allowed: set `allow_telemetry` to false and `disable_analytics` to true
  there if the design is not public.
- Freerouting leaves single connections open on a dense board and more passes do not close
  them (40 of 500 on a 287-part, six-layer board; two neighbouring header pins of one net
  among them). Call `b.route_open(BUILD)` after `autoroute()`: it connects one open
  connection at a time on a grid, first where a free path exists, then by moving unlocked
  tracks that find another way. On that board it closed 44 of 48, and all 13 on a 91-part
  board. What it reports as failed has no free path: look there, do not add passes.
  `remove_redundant()` and `straighten()` go before it, `widen()` after it.
- Count open connections with `b.unrouted()` after `b.fill_zones()`. Before the planes are
  filled every plane pin counts as open (173 instead of 40), and the "unrouted" number in
  Freerouting's log counts something else again.
- A fine-pitch part needs via sites around it. A QFN controller placed on the back under the
  wide switch-node copper of the front had none within 3 mm: four of its pins stayed open
  whatever routed them. Draw the obstacle map before blaming the router (`finish.obstacles()`
  returns a plane per layer and one for vias).
- Do not route the wide nets first to "give them room". With `route_open(only=POWER,
  locked=True)` before the autorouter, a 0.6 mm track ran past the controller's pins 0.2 mm
  from the pad ends and shut them in: 32 connections open instead of 9.
- For the last few open connections, rework the saved board instead of running the whole
  board again: `Board.reopen(pcb, ...)`, `b.rip_up(x0, y0, x1, y1)` around the spot, then
  `b.route_open(tmp, only=[net])` for the connection that stayed open and `b.route_open(tmp)`
  for what was taken out. Each try takes minutes and starts from the same board; whole-board
  runs with changed placement left a different handful open every time (2, 6, 3, 9). Try a
  few rectangles and orders in parallel and keep the best.
- Where a pin row is shut in, draw its exits by hand (locked) and let `route_open()` do the
  long part. Look first: on a QFN with 0.5 mm pitch one pin's via sat on the next pin's way
  out. Find the via sites before drawing: pads of the parts on the other side and wide
  tracks on inner layers left one row of sites 0.45 mm high above that pin row. Three vias
  there for three pins, the other two pins leave on tracks above them.
- Route with `b.negotiate(BUILD, step=0.05)` (leiterbahn-route, needs `cargo`) rather than with
  Freerouting. On a 65 x 56 mm six-layer board with 287 parts Freerouting left about 40
  connections open, `route_open()` closed all but 5, and which 5 depended on the order of
  the nets: a QFN's pins were shut in by whichever track came first. leiterbahn-route routes all nets
  at once and closed all 451 in 92 s. Use a 0.05 mm grid with 0.5 mm pitch parts: at 0.1 mm
  8 of 184 connections on a second board found no way at their class width. Call
  `route_open()` after it for what it reports open, then `improve()` and `widen()`.
  `autoroute()` stays for comparison.
- Before moving parts to shorten a net, look at `negotiate()`'s `by_net`: (cost, alone) per
  net. Long and equally long alone on the board (gate drives of 14 to 23 mm on one board):
  the pads are far apart, move the parts. Much longer than alone (a slope-compensation net
  of 29 mm against 14): other nets are in its way. Then `weights={net: 4}` helps if one or
  two nets are concerned; where thirteen nets at one controller were all longer than alone,
  weights gained nothing and routing them first left five other connections open: the area
  is too full, and the parts around it have to make room.
- Start a board script with `repeatable()` (from `leiterbahn.kicad.board`). Without it two runs
  of one script differ: the finishing pass left other connections open (VIN and a slope pin
  in one run, a switch node in the next), and hand rework written for the first result did
  not fit the second. Check it once: run the script twice and compare the boards.
- `b.improve()` after the board is closed: every autorouted run is searched again and
  replaced if shorter. Expect little on a dense board (2 % of the track length, 10 of 288
  vias there): each run is near its shortest with the others in place. Before spending time
  on routing order or better routers, measure what is left to gain: route each run with all
  other movable copper taken out (17 % there), and compare the half-perimeter length of the
  nets after swapping parts of one footprint (6 %). When both are small the board is at the
  limit of its area, and a long sensitive net is a placement matter: its part has to be
  beside its pin before routing starts.

## Speed

`autoroute()` runs several Freerouting strategies in parallel and caches sessions by a hash
of the DSN. A rerun with unchanged placement and hand routes imports the cached result in
seconds. If a run takes minutes, something changed the DSN.
